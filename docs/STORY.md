# The pigeon and the tea stall

*A short story about why Pigeon and Hive exist. The people are made up; everything they do works today.*

---

Leyla sells tea at the corner of a covered market. Thirty glasses before noon, forty after. On the wall behind her samovar there is a piece of paper with a Nimiq address on it, written in her nephew's careful hand:

`NQ42 8F3K ...`

Thirty-six characters. Nobody has ever read it out loud to the end.

A student from the university holds up his phone. "Can I pay with this?"
Leyla points at the paper. The student starts typing, stops, looks at the queue behind him, and puts a coin on the counter instead.

That night her nephew, Deniz, sits with the paper in his hand. He has sent money to his mother in another city twice this year, and both times he checked every group of four letters like a man defusing a bomb. Money is supposed to be a letter, he thinks. You write a name on the envelope and it arrives.

So he gives the address a name.

He opens Pigeon inside Nimiq Pay and types `leyla`. Nobody has taken it. He registers it, and the chain remembers: `leyla.nim` now points to the address on the wall. No contract, no company holding the list. A note on the chain that anyone can read.

The next morning he tapes a new paper over the old one. One word.

**leyla.nim**

The student comes back. He types five letters, sees Leyla's Identicon, the small shape that belongs only to her wallet, and sends the price of a glass. The pigeon flies. Leyla's phone buzzes before the tea has cooled.

By the end of the week a regular asks if the stall has a menu online. Deniz opens Hive. `leyla.nim` already has a cell waiting: a page, a small shop, a QR till. He uploads a page with four kinds of tea and a photo of the samovar. Now the name opens a place, not just a wallet.

At the counter, Leyla types 12 into the till and turns the screen around. A tourist scans it. The screen turns green: paid. Nobody asked for an address. Nobody checked thirty-six characters. The money found its way the way a letter does, because it had a name on it.

In the hive window, among the other cells, there is one more small hexagon now. It shows a tea glass and a number: the people who came by today.

Leyla doesn't know what a blockchain is. She doesn't need to. She knows that when someone asks how to pay her, she no longer points at a paper on the wall.

She says her name.

---

[Pigeon](https://pigeon.heylogram.com) gives Nimiq addresses names. [Hive](../hive/README.md) gives every name a home. Both are open source under MIT, in this repository.
